# Plane Trip: design summary

The living record of design decisions that are not vocabulary. Vocabulary lives in `CONTEXT.md`; hard-to-reverse architectural calls live in `docs/adr/`; findings about the original game live in `docs/research/`.

## Pitch

A single-player, side-view aerial combat homage to Triplane Turmoil. Two sides, Red and Blue. The player flies missions with objectives, dogfights AI pilots, bombs ground targets, and lands at the home airfield to rearm. Built in Godot 4.7 with GDScript. The web is the main target; desktop builds come free.

## Scope

In scope: single-player missions, one airframe with a Loadout the player builds, machine gun and bombs, fuel, AI pilots, anti-aircraft and flak guns, mission select with local progress.

Out of scope for now: multiplayer (its own effort later), infantry and capturing structures, radar, online anything, a custom level editor (the Godot editor is the level editor).

## Planes and Loadouts

- One airframe. Its fixed numbers are one data resource: 6 Slots, empty mass 200 (the original's units, as before), and a base the bare plane always has of 30 s of fuel, 40 rounds and 25 hit points. The base is there so a plane with no Slots filled is a fast, lightly armed option rather than a trap.
- A Fitting is a data resource, one per kind; the Refit screen lists what it finds. Starting table, all tuning starts (2026-10-18): Bomb Rack +2 bombs, weight 24; Ammunition Box +50 rounds, weight 6; Fuel Tank +20 s, weight 12; Armour Plate +8 hit points, weight 40. A Loadout is a count per Fitting, at most 6 in all. Today's Fighter is two racks, one box, two tanks.
- Weight slows the plane: top speed and acceleration scale by one minus 0.2 times load over empty mass, so a mixed full load is about 10 percent slower and six Armour Plates about 25 percent. Turn rate is untouched. Only bombs lighten in flight, for every plane: a Bomb Rack weighs its bombs and the weight goes as they drop, so a bomber that has dropped is faster. Fuel, rounds and shredded Armour keep their weight; the first two were too small to read in the original and lighter when shot is backwards.
- Enemies fly presets: Loadout resources in data that a mission names, say a bomber's racks and tanks and a fighter's boxes.
- History: four Plane Types named Scout, Fighter, Bomber and Heavy, with the original nations' stats as speed, acceleration and turn multipliers, were the player's choice at launch from milestone three until 2026-10-18, when the Loadout replaced them. The player builds the plane instead of picking one.

## Flight

- The model is Newtonian (ADR 0004): velocity is a vector and the nose is only where the plane points. Forces: propeller thrust along the nose (power over speed, capped at a static 40 m/s²), gravity 9 m/s² (playtest window 7 to 10), quadratic parasitic drag set so top speed is 72 m/s and a full-throttle vertical dive tops out at 1.5 times that, induced drag from lift squared, and lift perpendicular to the velocity from the angle of attack, linear to 18 degrees.
- The stick rotates the nose at 122 degrees per second but cannot hold more than the stall angle, so a full pull is maximum lift. The wing stalls, lift collapsing to 40 percent, when that maximum cannot carry the plane, i.e. below the stall speed of 18 m/s. A weathervane settles the nose, at 10 degrees per second, on the trim attitude that gives one G, so hands off is level flight; stalled, it settles on the velocity itself at 60 degrees per second, dropping the nose.
- Turn rate rises with speed to the corner at 70 percent of top speed (about 80 degrees per second, 7.7 G) and falls beyond it (55 at top speed, 29 at stall speed). Climbs cost what dives pay. Closing the throttle slows the plane by drag alone. The air brake (up and down together) adds 12 m/s² of drag.
- The thrust shape is a deliberate trade-off: two seconds to top speed and large dive gains cannot both come from a force model, so this one is punchy early, slow over the last 20 percent, and rewards dives.
- Numbers live in `data/flight_tuning.tres` (`FlightTuning`), physics only. The Loadout's weight scales top speed and acceleration (see Planes and Loadouts); until 2026-10-18 the Plane Types carried multipliers from the original's ratios instead.
- Kept from the original: the half-roll, the landing cone with no speed check, the ground roll with the tail settling, the stop speed, the Spin on death, the gun's rate of fire and the hit box. Bullets fly half again as long as the original's 1.15 s, for reach (2026-10-18).
- Hold-to-thrust, binary, no ramp, no toggle option. Fuel burns only while thrusting.
- Roll is a short half-roll that flips the plane, swaps pitch direction, and moves the bomb release side and the wheel position.
- Planes pass through each other. Debris from destroyed planes and targets damages planes.
- Takeoff is an active thing: the stick works at any speed on the runway and there is no clamp. A tail-down plane gets 40 percent of level acceleration, so push to level the tail, roll to rotation speed (40 percent of top speed), then pull. The runway holds the wheels until then. The nose touching the runway is a nose-over crash. With no input the tail settles at low speed as in the original.
- History (all 2026-10-17): the faithful original model felt sluggish and could not sustain a climb; a scalar model with fading thrust, weak drag and free turns followed; the Newtonian model was built as an experiment beside it, preferred in play, and the scalar model was removed.
- The camera shows about 20 plane lengths across (the original showed 16). Wider views make the same speed read as slower.
- Starting scale: world 130 plane lengths wide (120 until 2026-10-18, when 40 m of room was added between each airfield and its Border), 20 tall; screen shows about 20 by 11.5 at 16:9. One world unit is one metre. These are tuning starts, not rules.

## Damage and death

- Hit points are a single pool per plane.
- Final blow decides the death:
  - Explode: Bomb, Flak Gun burst, terrain, Water, Border. A Blast also finishes a plane already in a Spin.
  - Spin: Machine Gun bullets, Anti-Aircraft Gun bullets, Debris.
- In a Spin: reduced pitch rate, no thrust, no roll, no air brake (until 2026-10-19 the brake out-pulled gravity and could hold a dying plane up for good), guns and bombs still fire. Hitting anything kills the plane, a runway included: a Spin cannot be landed.
- Kills: the player is credited with a kill when their Machine Gun or Bomb takes an enemy plane out of the fight, into a Spin or outright, and once per plane. A plane the ground guns, Debris or the terrain bring down is nobody's kill, as in the original, where only the shooter scored.
- Debris: a destroyed plane or target throws up to 30 pieces with its velocity plus a random spread of about 20 m/s each way, as in the original. Pieces fly under the flight gravity, settle where they meet the terrain, and vanish after 4 s. A piece touching a plane wounds it with the original's roll. Debris is simulated like the other flying objects (it was rigid bodies in the scene until 2026-10-18), so every damage rule runs inside the deterministic step.
- Knockback: a hit shoves the plane. A bullet or a piece of Debris adds 0.25 m/s per point of damage to the plane's velocity along its own direction of flight; a Blast adds up to 12 m/s away from its centre, falling off like its damage to nothing at 16 m. Velocity only, never the nose, and the same for every plane in the air; a parked plane is not shoved, having nowhere to go but into the runway. A full-damage bullet moves a plane at cruising speed by about 2.75 m/s, enough to feel, not enough to aim with. The shove lands before the Debris of a plane it destroys is thrown, so the pieces carry it.
- A plane in a Spin is out of the fight but still in the world: guns stop aiming at it, and bullets that do strike it stop at it and are felt and seen but do nothing to it (they flew through it until 2026-10-18), while a Bomb or a Shell touching it is a Blast and destroys it. A destroyed plane is gone from the world; nothing strikes or damages it again.

## World

- Heightfield terrain with Water segments (ADR 0003). Terrain and Water are fatal on contact except for a valid landing.
- Borders (left and right) are fatal. The Ceiling is soft: beyond it the controls lock, lift is gone, the plane tumbles and its nose turns toward the ground, and that continues for 2 s after it is back under. Nothing visible pushes it back; once it is fully off the screen, 5 m above the Ceiling, its climb is cancelled so it does not linger out of sight, and gravity brings it down. The tumble runs at three half-rolls per second and the plane comes out inverted after an odd number, so the pilot has to read which way is up. The original's push-back and random spin, and a recoverable-stall variant, were tried on 2026-10-17 and dropped.
- Rendered in 3D over a 2D simulation (ADR 0001), far perspective camera with a narrow field of view, follows the player with lookahead, fixed zoom, clamped to the World with the view's top 12 m under the Ceiling, so a plane can climb a little past the top of the screen before the Ceiling takes it.
- Behind the action is the Backdrop (ADR 0006): a tree line, hills, clouds and mountains at increasing depth on a ground sheet that runs to the horizon, laid out from a seed to cover whatever the camera can see and faded toward the sky with depth. It is dressing, outside the simulation; it is there so the plane's movement can be read at any altitude, including at the Ceiling with the ground out of view. The Mountains always put a peak at the top of the view at the Ceiling, and the Clouds give the eye something between the peaks; they drift slowly and wrap at the layer's ends. The trees on the slab and in the tree line sway in the wind through one vertex shader, a windmill or two turn their sails in the tree line, a factory stands in front of the hills with its chimney smoking, smoke columns rise from wreck sites in the hills, and on a mission on the front line (a toggle on the Backdrop, on for Hold the Line) distant guns flash on the hills and mountains a few times a minute and leave a puff that lingers: silent, small and far, so they are never taken for a Shell burst. Backdrop motion runs on the render clock in the Backdrop's own process step, pauses with the game, and takes its timing from the seed.

## Airfields and rearming

- Landing follows the original rule exactly: wheels within the runway, nose between 20 degrees up and 10 degrees down, right side up. No speed check. Anything else is a crash. A hard-landing damage rule is a candidate later addition.
- Any plane can land on any Airfield. Only the plane's own Side's Airfield rearms it.
- The player's Rearm comes with a Refit (2026-10-18; it was an automatic refill over 4 s before). The Refit screen opens at mission start in place of the plane choice, on the player's spawn after each loss, and when the player's Plane stops on its own Airfield having flown since its last Refit, so a plane sitting on the runway after a confirm does not reopen it. The World runs on behind it, as in the original: a bomber can catch you on the runway and a Protect objective can fail while you dither, and the time on the screen is the cost of the Rearm. The one exception is the Refit at mission start, which holds the World until the first confirm (2026-10-19): with the first enemy launched at zero seconds, the player read the briefing while it crossed the map, and with nobody flying both original missions were lost at 18 s. Confirming is instant and sets the Plane back at the start of its runway, pointing down it, as the original's mechanic pushed it out of the hangar (2026-10-19; it used to sit where it had stopped, often at the far end with no room to take off). There is no cancel: the screen owns the keys, so the plane cannot taxi off under it; Back exists only at mission start, where it returns to the mission list; pause stacks on top and resumes back into the Refit.
- The World holds the player's Loadout. A confirm sets it: a Plane parked on its own Airfield is Rearmed at once, a dead or spawning one is built from it at spawn. So being destroyed with the screen open needs no special case; the choice applies to the next plane.
- The screen is rows, one per Fitting, left and right adjusting its count, a slot gauge of used over total, and a readout of the resulting plane: speed and climb as percent of empty, bombs, rounds, fuel in seconds, hull. The last Loadout chosen in this session is the default, so confirming is one key press. A picture of the plane with its racks is a later art pass.
- AI planes keep the automatic Rearm over 4 s, to their authored Loadout; the seconds stand in for the screen, and the parked bomber is a target meanwhile.
- On the runway with the throttle closed the wheel brakes add 6 m/s² to the 2 m/s² of rolling friction, so a landing at 30 m/s stops in about 60 m (2026-10-18; rolling alone needed over 200 m and the plane ran off the end).

## Missions

- Objective types: Destroy (a target must die), Protect (fails if the target dies), Return (land at the Home Airfield), Shoot Down (a count of enemy Planes by the player's own gun or Bombs, 2026-10-19; also complete once the enemy is grounded, its Hangar gone or its pool spent with nothing Aloft, so bombing the Hangar is the other way to finish it; kills by Wingmen or ground guns never count, and a Wingman the player downs is nobody's). Each is required or optional. The objective slip shows a Shoot Down's tally while it is open.
- Win when every required objective is complete, whether the player is in the air or not. Lose when a Protect objective fails or the player's plane stock is exhausted. Default stock is three planes, set per mission.
- Player respawns on the ground at the Home Airfield after a short delay.
- Each Side's Hangar sends up a Squadron (2026-10-19): the definition says how many Planes it keeps Aloft at once, which Loadout preset each Aloft slot flies (a replacement flies what the Plane it replaces flew, a shorter list repeats its last entry, an empty one copies the player), how many it may send in total (0 for no limit) and the interval between any two launches on that Side so they do not leave in a clump. The first launch on either side waits for the mission's first-launch delay (5 s in every mission, so the player is not caught on the runway), a replacement comes after the respawn delay or the interval, whichever is later. Destroying a Hangar stops its Squadron. Wingmen are the player's Side's Squadron, none by default, from the Home Airfield's Hangar; friendly fire is as for everything else, since a hit does not check sides. (Until 2026-10-19 one enemy flew at a time from a finite pool.)
- Ground defenders: Anti-Aircraft Guns (bursts of six rounds with lead, inside an upward cone, within 60 m) and Flak Guns (one shell every 0.9 s on a 1.3 to 1.7 s fuse, bursting on contact, 80 damage within 4 m falling to nothing at 16 m). Buildings and Hangars are passive Ground Targets and fall only to bombs; guns can also be shot up. Bombs fall under the flight gravity from the belly with the plane's velocity, released from where the Bomb hangs on the model, 1.4 m under the fuselage (the original released 3.6 m down, past the wheels; changed 2026-10-18 so the falling Bomb starts where the hanging one was); one bomb destroys whatever it lands on and explodes a plane it touches. Bombs release at most one every 0.1 s (0.5 s until 2026-10-18, which felt slow), so holding the key lays a spaced stick; the first press releases at once.
- A Protect objective counts as satisfied while its target stands; it only ever turns to failed. Ground targets snap to the terrain height where they are placed. Ground fire ignores its own side's targets so a gun's rounds do not stop in its own footprint.
- AI pilots port the original state machine: take off, patrol, turn at Borders, look ahead to avoid terrain, chase within range, fire within a cone unless a friendly is in the way, simulate the bomb drop every tick and release over enemy structures that none of their own falling bombs is already headed for, go home when out of ordnance or low on fuel. Deviations, all because our flight model keeps speed through climbs and turns wide at speed: speed thresholds are fractions of top speed; the turn-around slows down with the engine off and the air brake before looping, up if there is room and down after a half-roll otherwise, and starts at the distance that needs; the ceiling guard accounts for the loop radius; a pilot wanting home flies to the approach point and turns around when heading away; the approach steers for the touchdown point and holds speed with brake and throttle; takeoff is push, roll, pull.
- Airfields have a landing direction. A field near a Border should face away from it so planes do not have to turn around right after take-off.
- Levels are Godot scenes: terrain as an editable curve with a tool script that rebuilds the mesh, airfields and targets as placed scenes, mission rules (objectives, stock, enemy pool) in an inspector-edited Resource.
- The mission list is a list of entries, each pointing at the mission's scene and its MissionDefinition; the title and briefing are authored once, on the definition, and the Refit screen at mission start shows the briefing. The definition names the enemy's Loadout preset. An Objective naming a Ground Target the scene does not have is reported when the World loads.
- Progression: all missions available, completion tracked, saved locally in the user directory as `progress.json`. Four missions, in order: Clear the Sky (2026-10-19; one Red Interceptor at a time without limit, shoot down three, guns on both sides, the player defaults to the Interceptor), Strike the Depot (destroy under flak; Red flies three Interceptors at a time from a pool of six, two Wingmen fly for Blue; until 2026-10-19 its "fighters" carried bombs and the mission had a Protect on the fuel store, so it opened with a bomber run on the base), Hold the Line (protect the fuel store, destroy their hangar; Red keeps three Aloft from a pool of nine, two bombers and an Interceptor escort, two Wingmen fly for Blue, and since 2026-10-19 an Anti-Aircraft Gun and a Flak Gun stand by the store, which the AI bombs first) and The Big Show (2026-10-19; Red keeps five Aloft, three Interceptors and two bombers, four Wingmen on Interceptors fly for Blue, shoot down six; no Protect, the bombers' threat is the Home Hangar, which stops the Wingmen). The two aerial missions came from a playtest where the opening enemy's bombs on the base made the first mission too hard; with nobody flying, both original missions were lost at 18 s before the first-launch delay, the held Refit and the Blue guns, and Hold the Line holds about 35 s after them.

## Presentation

- The look of everything that is not the picture of the World is one voice, chosen 2026-10-19 (ADR 0008): paper in the operations hut for the menus, instruments for flight, in the Second World War. One palette for both (`UiPalette`: olive drab, khaki, cream, black ink, a red stamp, dull brass), flat like the models; one Theme built in code (`UiTheme`) from three open-licensed fonts, a stencil (Allerta Stencil) for headings, the wordmark and the Bands, a typewriter (Special Elite) for everything typed on paper, and a condensed grotesque (Oswald) for the dials' small labels. Wording on screen uses the glossary; the period voice is in the type and the layout, not in flavour copy. The one exception is HULL for hit points, which fits a dial.
- The Instrument Panel shows fuel, rounds and hull as round dials with eased needles and a red arc at the low end on a riveted olive plate at the bottom left, with bombs and planes left as silhouette rows beside them; a steady red lamp lights beside the fuel and hull dials when they are in the red (no blinking: a lit lamp is a glance, a blink under fire is noise). The objective list is a cream slip pinned top left, each line with an ink box ticked red when complete and crossed when failed, so it reads over sky, ground and smoke. Nothing floats over the view in white. Mission messages are set on a Band: the outcome in large stencil for 2.5 s before the Debrief, an objective turning in a smaller one. No radar. The Instrument Panel reads simulation state and never writes it. (Until 2026-10-19 the HUD was white labels and stock progress bars at the top left, unreadable over the pale sky.)
- Menus are Sheets: the title has a large stencil wordmark over the airfield scene with a small slip for Start and Quit; the mission list is one index card per mission, its title, its objective and plane counts, and a red COMPLETE Stamp once won; the Refit is a requisition form, a line per Fitting with its count adjusted by the buttons beside it, a row of Slot boxes filled as used, a typed readout, and the Plane itself turning in a viewport with one Bomb model hung per bomb in the Loadout (other Fittings hang nothing yet: their models are an art pass; in flight the plane still hangs one Bomb, a Picture rule left alone); the Debrief is a report sheet on which the outcome slams down as a Stamp with a thunk and a twitch of the paper, over the objective list, kills, planes lost and the mission's time; the pause is a small slip. The World behind the Refit, the Debrief and the pause is dimmed about 30 percent, so a shape moving behind the paper still carries the cost of the time spent there.
- Behind the title and the mission list is the airfield scene: the player's Plane parked tail-down before its Hangar with the depot beyond and the Backdrop's hills and mountains behind, seen low from three-quarters along the runway, the propeller idling and the camera pushing in a couple of metres over two minutes. Built from the game's own nodes and models; nothing in it is a World.
- The game opens on a plain cream page with one line of type and a bar, drawn with nothing the engine has not compiled already, so the browser's own loader leaves as soon as the engine runs; the game scene and everything it reaches loads on a background thread behind the bar (2026-10-19). Then a loading Sheet in the operations hut, READYING stamped on it, with a ruled bar and a line naming what is being readied: the paper and type, the airfield, guns bombs and smoke, the wireless. Behind it the airfield scene is built and every shader a mission uses is compiled (2026-10-19; the web build had sat on the engine's own loader at 100 percent for the whole wait, and then froze at each effect's first use). Then the title.
- The sound switch (2026-10-19): one button on the title and the pause slips, "Sound: on" or "Sound: off", and M anywhere, mutes the Master bus and so everything; the choice is saved beside the progress as `settings.cfg`. Separate music and effects levels would be a settings screen, which there is not.
- Screen flow: title, mission list, Refit, mission (with the Refit again on every spawn and every stop at home), debrief with retry, back to the list. Pause with restart during a mission. Every change of screen comes in through a short fade from black; the switch itself is instant. Menus walk with the keys or the pad's stick, and Escape or B is Back wherever a screen has a Back (the mission list to the title, the Refit at mission start to the mission list, the pause to resume), with a heavy red focus ring showing what has the keys. The World stops while paused (it ran on under the pause menu until 2026-10-19).
- Audio (ADR 0009, 2026-10-19): every sound is synthesised by a script and built to a WAV, none recorded. The engine is a radial's firing at 36 pulses a second at rest, spooled over about half a second toward what the throttle asks (idle with it closed, higher with speed), sputtering in a Spin, silent when the Plane is gone; the air over the airframe rises with speed and the wheels rumble on the runway. Coughs on the last six seconds of fuel; the airflow buffets in a stall and howls in a Spin; wheels meeting a runway chirp and thump. Guns: the Machine Gun's shot, the lighter higher Anti-Aircraft Gun's, the Flak Gun's heavy report, a clunk as a Bomb leaves its rack, and every Bomb in the air whistling from where it is, higher the faster it falls (six at once at most). Blasts: the Bomb's, the Shell's crump, a Ground Target coming down, a Plane into the Water. Rounds ending: a strike on a Plane's frame, the heavier one the player hears on their own, a ricochet off a Ground Target, a thud into the ground, a plink into the Water. Shots, hits and impacts are pitched a few percent either way from a seeded source so a burst is not one sound repeated. Everything is heard from the player's Plane, or from where it was lost while the player is dead, fading to nothing over 300 m, and placed on the action plane so the camera pans it. The menus: a tick as focus moves, a typewriter clack on a choice and a lower one going back, a thunk as a Stamp lands; the typewriter's bell when an objective or the mission turns complete and a dull clank when one fails; wind with a distant engine over the airfield behind the title. Four buses, Master with a limiter, World, UI and Music; the World drops 12 dB under the Debrief and the pause. Music (2026-10-19): a dance-band tune, "Field Orders", sixteen bars of medium swing in F played by a synthesised band (a reedy lead, a muted horn, an upright bass, a comping guitar, brushed drums) and put through a "wireless", a narrow band with overdrive, flutter, mains hum and crackle, so it comes out of a valve radio in the operations hut. It plays behind the title and the mission list, fading in and out with the screens, and never in flight. The Debrief gets a sting as the Stamp lands: a flourish for complete, a sombre falling phrase for failed, through the same wireless. (Until 2026-10-19 the engine, the gun and one explosion at three pitches were generated in code at start-up and impacts were silent.)
- Art: low-poly flat-coloured models built from measurements by Blender scripts and exported to glTF (ADR 0005, `docs/modelling.md`), set in the Second World War. One plane model, a Fokker Dr.I-like triplane as the nod to the original, its body in the Side's colour and its propeller turning with the engine. Ground Targets: a brick depot with a flag, an arched hangar with its doors in the Side's colour, a quad anti-aircraft gun and a heavy flak gun in sandbag rings with pennants, and one ruin for anything destroyed. Each kind has its own palette so they read from across the screen. The Backdrop's trees, hills, mountains and clouds are models with two or three variants each in one script. The terrain slab is painted in field strips from a seed with a sandy shore along every stretch of Water, and sparse trees stand on its back half at the terrain's height, behind anything on the ground. Airfields are still placeholder geometry.
- Feel (milestone four, ADR 0007): modern game-feel kept inside the flat-colour low-poly language. Everything here is Picture, reacts to World events or reads World state, runs on the render clock in pooled MultiMeshes with fixed caps, and uses no lights, image assets or hit-stop.
  - Tracers: each bullet is one box stretched along its velocity, a 2.5 m by 0.3 m bright head and behind it a tail reaching back to where the round was 0.3 s ago, faded and tapered by a shader and clamped by the round's age so it never reaches past the muzzle. Ground gun rounds are tinted red so the player can tell whose fire is in the air.
  - Muzzle Flash on every gun, plane or ground: a 2 m glowing cone with a 0.7 m mouth, for 65 ms, solid until its last 40 percent, with a random roll, riding a plane's muzzle. (50 ms fading from the first frame could not be seen; 80 ms was too much.) Two brass Casings per Machine Gun shot, thrown toward the camera and back, tumbling under gravity for 1.5 s.
  - Impacts: a bullet ending on the ground raises a dust puff, on Water a splash spike, on a Ground Target a grey tick; a bullet striking a plane throws six warm 0.6 m sparks back off the impact point for 0.5 s, solid until their last 40 percent, beside the existing white body flash. Machine Gun and Anti-Aircraft rounds look the same when they land.
  - Burst: a white flash for 0.1 s, an orange sphere growing and fading over 0.5 s, six to ten grey puffs rising and fading over 2 s, and the simulated Debris. A Bomb, a falling Ground Target and a Plane that explodes or hits the ground get one, sized to the thing; a Shell gets a small grey one with no flash; a Plane into the Water gets one big splash; a Plane over the Border bursts like any other (it got nothing until the first playtest asked for it).
  - Damage smoke: a plane under half its hit points trails smoke that thickens and darkens to black at zero, so a Spin trails heavy smoke and a hurt enemy can be read from across the screen.
  - Wingtip ribbons: thin white ribbons from every wingtip, six on the triplane, under high g-load (3 to 6) or at high speed (85 to 110 percent of top), fading in over 0.2 s and out at half that rate so a reversal through zero g does not blank them, persisting 0.8 s behind the plane. They show on tight turns and fast dives, not in cruise.
  - Sway: mostly a slow wander of about 5 degrees, as in rough air, with a hint of bank, up to 4 degrees, into the pitch change at the full pitch rate. (15 degrees of bank with 2 of noise felt wrong in play; the balance was turned around.) Off during the half-roll, the Ceiling tumble and a Spin, which own the roll, and on the ground. Drawn on a pivot between the node and the body, derived from state so AI planes sway too.
  - Camera shake: trauma that decays over 0.6 s, strength trauma squared, moving the view with smooth noise up to 4 m and rolling it up to 2 degrees (1.5 m was a few pixels on a view 160 m wide). Fed by Blasts within 60 m scaled by distance (0.8 at the centre) and by taking a hit (0.5, 1.6 times that in a Spin). Trauma squared makes small shares vanish, so the sharp things are kicks: each of the player's shots jolts the view 0.6 m back along the nose, recovering in about 50 ms, and each hit jolts it 1.5 m the way the round was flying.
  - The Bomb is a model that hangs under the plane while there is one to drop, disappears when released and reappears when the next can be released, so an enemy's bombs left can be seen. The same model falls.
  - Shells: a Flak Gun's Shell is drawn 1.8 m long and glowing orange, leaving a pale puff every 0.05 s, so it can be watched climb and dodged.
  - Sounds: shell bursts and falling Ground Targets reuse the explosion sound pitched up. Impacts stay silent.
- Input: keyboard and gamepad through named input actions from day one. Keyboard defaults: Down pulls the nose up and Up pushes it down (stick convention, confirmed in the first playtest), Left or Right roll, Shift thrust, Z guns, X bombs. Gamepad details decided with a controller in hand.

## Engineering

- GDScript only. No threads or native extensions, to keep the web export simple.
- The simulation is pure GDScript with no nodes: inputs and a time step in, new state out. The World is one module (`World`) that owns the Planes with their Pilots, the projectiles, the Ground Targets and their guns, the Hangar's pool, the player's stock and the Mission's outcome; it is built from the World Map, the Ground Targets and the MissionDefinition and stepped once per physics tick. Flight model, combat, ground guns, damage and mission rules are its parts. One step returns events (a Plane spawned, hit or destroyed, a gun fired, a Bomb released, a Bomb or Shell burst, a bullet landing on ground, Water or a target, a target destroyed, the Mission ended) for the rendering to react to; an event carries what the picture needs to draw it, such as the muzzle point of a shot or the point and direction of a hit, and nothing a rule does not already know; what simply is (positions, projectiles, kills) is read from its fields. Nodes render it and feed it input; they decide nothing. Bullets, Bombs, Shells and Debris share one flying-object record and one gravity step; each kind adds only how it ends.
- Rules are tested through the World's interface with a map, targets and a mission built in code; scene tests only check that the placed nodes become a World and that nodes follow it.
- Tests with gdUnit4 (v6.2.2 at time of writing). Formatting and linting with gdtoolkit (4.5.0).
- Git with a GitHub remote. CI on every push to main (2026-10-19): lint and tests, the headless Web export from `export_presets.cfg`, then GitHub Pages (2026-10-28; until then, butler to itch.io). The export keeps threads. Pages cannot send the cross-origin isolation headers, so the build is a progressive web app whose service worker adds them. The test suite, the test framework, model and sound scripts and reports stay out of the build.

## Milestones

1. **Fly**: flight model, terrain, crash, camera, one Airfield, landing, automatic rearm, keyboard and gamepad input. Boots straight into a test World.
2. **Dogfight**: Machine Gun, hit points, Spin, Debris, one AI pilot from an enemy Hangar, placeholder engine and gun audio.
3. **Strike**: Bombs, buildings, Anti-Aircraft and Flak Guns, Objectives, win and lose, the first HUD, title, mission list, plane choice, debrief, pause, local save.
4. **Feel**: Tracers, Muzzle Flashes and Casings, impacts, Bursts with shake and the missing sounds, damage smoke and wingtip ribbons, the hanging Bomb with its release interval, Sway, knockback. One branch each, in that order, each accepted in play before the next.
5. **Refit**: the airframe and Fittings as data, Loadouts in the World with weight on the flight model, the Refit screen at launch, on spawn and at home, enemy presets, Plane Types removed.
6. **Paper and instruments**: the UI's voice (ADR 0008): fonts, palette and Theme, the Instrument Panel, the airfield scene behind the title, Sheets for the mission list, the Refit, the Debrief and the pause, Stamps and Bands, fades, focus and Back on the pad, UI sounds and buses, the World stopping while paused, the mission clock. Built in one pass and reviewed screen by screen.

Later: hard-landing damage, full audio, art pass, CI and deploy, multiplayer as a separate effort.
